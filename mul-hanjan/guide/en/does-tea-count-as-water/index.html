Does tea count as water intake?

Tea, coffee and sparkling water, explained simply.

Published 2026-11-21

Quick answer: Generally, yes. Daily water guidelines are about total water: plain water, other drinks and the water in food. Tea, coffee and sparkling water are mostly water, so they are generally said to count toward your daily fluids. Plain water is still a good main drink.

A lot of people feel guilty about their morning coffee or afternoon iced tea, as if it "doesn't count." Here's what US health sources generally say.

What the guidelines actually measure

In the US, the main reference is the National Academies' Dietary Reference Intakes for Water, Potassium, Sodium, Chloride, and Sulfate (report released in 2004). It sets an Adequate Intake for total water:

Menabout 3.7 Labout 125 oz
Womenabout 2.7 Labout 91 oz

The key word is total. These numbers include water from all beverages and from food. Mayo Clinic notes that food usually provides around 20% of daily fluid intake, and the rest comes from drinks. So the amount you need to drink is less than the totals above.

These are general reference values, not personal targets. Your needs change with your size, activity, the weather, and your health.

Tea

Tea is almost entirely water. Herbal teas and caffeine-free teas (like rooibos, chamomile, or Korean barley tea) are generally treated much like water. Black and green tea contain some caffeine, but in normal amounts they are generally said to still add to your fluid intake.

Watch for: sweet tea and bottled teas with a lot of added sugar.

Coffee

Caffeine has a mild diuretic effect (it can make you pee a bit more). Still, for people who drink coffee regularly, coffee is generally said to contribute to daily fluid intake rather than cancel it out. Mayo Clinic also notes that caffeinated drinks can count toward your daily water.

Watch for: how much caffeine you have in a day, and creamers or syrups that add sugar. If caffeine affects your sleep or heart rate, it makes sense to cut back.

Sparkling water

Plain sparkling water is water with carbonation added, so it is generally counted the same as still water. If it helps you drink more, that's a win.

Watch for: flavored versions with added sugar, and mineral waters that are high in sodium. Check the label.

What about other drinks?

  • Milk is mostly water and generally counts.
  • Juice and soda add fluid, but also sugar. Fine sometimes, not ideal as your main drink.
  • Alcohol is usually best left out of your water count.

A note on health

This page is general information, not medical advice. If you have a heart, kidney or other health condition, are pregnant, or have been told to limit fluids or caffeine, talk to a doctor about how much and what to drink.

FAQ

Does tea count toward daily water intake?

Generally, yes. Tea is mostly water, and health sources generally say it counts toward your total fluid intake. Herbal and caffeine-free teas are an easy choice.

Does coffee dehydrate you?

Coffee has a mild diuretic effect, but for regular coffee drinkers it is generally said to still add to total fluid intake. Plain water is still a good main drink.

Does sparkling water count as water?

Generally, yes. Plain sparkling water is usually counted like still water. Check the label for added sugar or sodium.

How much total water do adults need?

The National Academies set an Adequate Intake of about 3.7 liters a day for men and 2.7 liters for women. That's total water from all drinks and food, not plain water alone. For a quick sense of what 2 liters looks like, see How many cups is 2 liters of water?

Do alcoholic drinks count?

It is usually best not to count alcohol toward your water goal.
