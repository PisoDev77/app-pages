Count your water in glasses, not ounces

One glass, one tap. No math.

Published 2026-11-21

Quick answer: Counting water in glasses is easier than adding up milliliters or ounces. Measure your usual glass once, turn your daily goal into a number of glasses (2 liters is about 8½ cups of 8 oz), and then just count: "I've had 4, I need 4 more."

Most people who stop tracking water don't stop because they don't care. They stop because adding up "12 oz here, 16.9 oz there" gets tiring. A simple count is easier to keep going.

Why glasses work better than numbers

  • Easy to remember. "Five glasses" sticks in your head. "1,420 ml" doesn't.
  • Easy to see. You can picture what's left: three more glasses before bed.
  • No math in the moment. You don't need to read the label on every bottle. One glass is one glass.
  • Good enough. For everyday tracking, being off by a few ounces matters much less than drinking regularly through the day.

Step 1: Pick your glass and measure it once

Use the glass, mug or bottle you reach for most. Fill it the way you usually do (most people don't fill to the brim), then pour it into a measuring cup. That's your glass size.

Step 2: Tie each glass to something you already do

A new habit is easier to keep when it rides on an old one. Instead of "drink more water," try "one glass every time I…":

  • …wake up (keep a glass by the sink or bed)
  • …make coffee in the morning
  • …sit down at my desk
  • …finish a meeting or a class
  • …eat lunch and dinner
  • …get home
  • …brush my teeth at night

That's already 8 glasses. Pick the ones that fit your day and skip the rest.

Step 3: Put the count where you can see it

Out of sight, out of mind. A home screen widget shows how many glasses are left every time you unlock your phone, and you can log a glass right from the widget without opening an app.

Step 4: Use reminders, but gently

Reminders help on busy days, when you forget to drink until the afternoon. A few nudges at times you choose usually work better than one every hour. If a reminder feels annoying, move it to a routine moment, like right after lunch.

A few tips that make it stick

  • Start smaller than you think. If you drink 3 glasses now, aim for 5 first, not 10.
  • Keep your glass in sight. A glass on your desk gets used more than one in the cupboard.
  • Don't chase perfect. A missed day isn't a failure. Just start again tomorrow.

How much you need depends on your body, the weather and how active you are. If you have a health condition or need to limit fluids, talk to a doctor about the right amount for you.

FAQ

Is it accurate enough to count water in glasses?

For everyday tracking, yes. Measure your usual glass once, then count glasses. Being off by a few ounces a day matters much less than drinking regularly.

How many glasses of water is 2 liters?

About 8½ cups of 8 oz, about 5½ glasses of 12 oz, or four 16.9 oz bottles.

How do I find out how much my glass holds?

Fill it the way you normally do, then pour it into a measuring cup. Many drinking glasses hold somewhere around 8 to 16 oz.

What if I use different glasses during the day?

Pick the one you use most as your main glass. In One Glass you can set several cups (for example a cup, a tumbler and a bottle) and tap whichever one you drank from.

Do tea and coffee count as glasses?

Other drinks count toward your total fluid intake too. See Does tea count as water intake?
